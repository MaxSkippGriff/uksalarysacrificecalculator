Salary Sacrifice During Maternity Leave 2026/27

Published by the UK Money Calculators editorial team. Last updated for the 2026/27 tax year.

Salary sacrifice and maternity leave interact in ways that catch people off guard. Employer pension contributions, the effect on Statutory Maternity Pay, and what happens when you return — all of these are worth understanding before you go on leave.

Employer pension contributions during maternity leave

Employer pension contributions, including those arising through salary sacrifice, must continue during maternity leave in certain circumstances:

  • Ordinary Maternity Leave (weeks 1–26): If you receive contractual maternity pay (i.e. enhanced pay above SMP), the employer must continue pension contributions as if you were still working normally. The employer contribution is calculated on your usual salary — not your reduced maternity pay.
  • Additional Maternity Leave (weeks 27–52): The employer duty to continue pension contributions depends on whether you are receiving contractual pay. If you are only on SMP (or unpaid), the employer's obligation to contribute depends on your contract and scheme rules.

These rules come from the Equality Act 2010 and apply even if you are not making employee contributions during leave. Your employer cannot use your absence as a reason to reduce or stop contributions while contractual pay is being paid.

Employee contributions during maternity leave

You are not required to continue making employee pension contributions during maternity leave. The salary sacrifice arrangement is typically suspended when your salary falls too low to sustain it.

In practice:

  • Most payroll systems will automatically suspend salary sacrifice when pay drops below the sacrificed amount.
  • If your sacrifice exceeds your remaining pay, you will not be charged — the sacrifice simply doesn't occur for that period.
  • You can choose to make personal pension contributions independently during leave if you wish, though these would be regular member contributions (with tax relief via the pension scheme) rather than salary sacrifice.

When you return to work the arrangement should resume automatically. Check with payroll to confirm.

Does salary sacrifice affect Statutory Maternity Pay?

This is the area that catches most people out. SMP is calculated on average weekly earnings (AWE) over an 8-week reference period ending 15 weeks before the expected week of childbirth — the "qualifying week."

HMRC's position is that SMP should be based on the contractual salary — the cash-equivalent that would be paid without any sacrifice. But some employers calculate AWE on the post-sacrifice salary actually paid during the reference period. If that happens, your SMP could be lower than it should be.

  • If your sacrifice was active throughout the reference period, and your employer uses the post-sacrifice figure, your AWE (and hence SMP) could be reduced.
  • Some employment lawyers and advisers argue that where salary sacrifice forms part of the original contract redesign, the lower post-sacrifice salary is the "contractual salary" for SMP purposes — though this is contested.
  • The safest approach if you are planning maternity leave: check your employer's SMP calculation method before the qualifying week. You may wish to pause salary sacrifice before the reference period begins.

If you think your SMP has been undercalculated, raise it with your employer or HR. If unresolved, HMRC can adjudicate SMP disputes.

Statutory Maternity Pay: the basics

For 2026/27, SMP is paid for up to 39 weeks:

  • First 6 weeks: 90% of average weekly earnings (no cap)
  • Weeks 7–39: the lower of 90% of AWE or the flat weekly rate (£184.03/week for 2026/27, subject to confirmation)

To qualify, you must have worked for the same employer for at least 26 weeks into the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth, and your average weekly earnings must be at or above the Lower Earnings Limit (£123/week for 2026/27). Salary sacrifice doesn't affect eligibility as long as your post-sacrifice earnings stay above the LEL.

Many employers offer contractual maternity pay above SMP. Check your contract or staff handbook.

Annual allowance during maternity leave

Even if you make no pension contributions during maternity leave, the tax year still counts for carry-forward purposes. Unused annual allowance from previous years can be carried forward for up to three years and used when you return and are sacrificing at a higher rate.

Keep a record of pension input amounts in the years immediately before and after maternity leave. That way you can use any available carry-forward allowance when you return.

Frequently asked questions

Do I continue to get pension contributions during maternity leave?

Your employer must continue making pension contributions during ordinary maternity leave (the first 26 weeks) if you are receiving contractual maternity pay. The contributions are based on your normal salary, not your reduced maternity pay. During additional maternity leave (weeks 27–52), the employer's obligation depends on whether contractual pay is still being paid and what your scheme rules say.

Is my maternity pay reduced by salary sacrifice?

It can be, if your employer calculates SMP based on the post-sacrifice salary actually paid during the reference period. HMRC's intention is that SMP is calculated on contractual earnings, but practice varies. If you are concerned, check with your payroll team whether they use the pre-sacrifice or post-sacrifice salary to calculate your average weekly earnings — and consider pausing sacrifice before the SMP qualifying week if needed.

Can I restart salary sacrifice after maternity leave?

Yes. When you return to work, your salary sacrifice arrangement should resume. Confirm with your payroll team that it has been reactivated and that the correct amount is being sacrificed. If you want to increase your sacrifice when you return (for example to use carry-forward annual allowance), contact your payroll team or scheme administrator to update the amount.

Should I pause salary sacrifice before my maternity leave starts?

It depends on your employer's SMP calculation method. If your employer uses post-sacrifice earnings for the SMP reference period calculation, pausing sacrifice before the 8-week reference period begins could result in a higher SMP entitlement. This is most relevant for people on higher salaries where SMP amounts to 90% of AWE in the first six weeks. Seek advice if you are unsure.

What happens to my pension during unpaid maternity leave?

During unpaid additional maternity leave, the employer's obligation to continue pension contributions typically ceases unless your contract says otherwise. You remain a member of the pension scheme and your existing funds continue to grow. You can choose to make personal contributions if you wish, though not via salary sacrifice while unpaid.
